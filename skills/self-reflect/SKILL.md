# Self-reflect

Recover useful progress when an approach repeats without new evidence, a
correction invalidates an assumption, or the user requests reflection. Do not
add a checkpoint ritual to ordinary successful work.

1. Reconstruct the requested outcome, authority, actual changes, and unfinished
   acceptance from the existing task record and artifacts.
2. Name the failed assumption and the observation needed to resolve it.
3. Obtain the cheapest discriminating observation or take the next authorized
   action. Change approach rather than repeating an unchanged failed call.
4. Continue useful work. Report an exact blocker only after checking supported
   alternatives and recoverable prerequisites within authority.

Keep reflection brief and conversational: what changed your understanding and
what you will do next. No prescribed fields, new task database, or obligatory
review agent. For difficult causal investigation, use `systematic-debugging`;
for completion evidence, use `verify-work`. A prior completion statement is not
execution evidence. Missing telemetry remains unknown.

Preserve the objective across handoffs and record material failures and gaps
when interruption requires a durable note. Respect read-only requests; recovery
does not grant mutation, publication, or wider scope. Propose recurring lessons
when useful, but do not silently edit skills or global instructions.
