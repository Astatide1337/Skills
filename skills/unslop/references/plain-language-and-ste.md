# Plain language and ASD-STE100

Use this reference selectively. It is a local adaptation of public guidance,
not a reproduction of either standard and not a certification of compliance.
The existing `unslop` procedure remains authoritative for preserving meaning,
voice, evidence, and uncertainty.

## Choose the standard by destination

| Destination | Apply | Do not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chat, explanations, issues, plans, reports, and ordinary documentation | Plain-language principles aligned with [ISO 24495-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html) | Do not force a controlled vocabulary or a maintenance-manual tone |
| Procedures, runbooks, maintenance, safety instructions, or text that must translate consistently | Plain language plus an [ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about.html)-informed technical pass | Do not describe the result as STE-compliant unless the full specification was deliberately applied and reviewed |
| Mixed documents | Apply the relevant approach section by section | Do not flatten a personal or explanatory section into procedural prose |

## Plain-language baseline

Plain language is reader-first. Before rewriting, identify what the reader
needs to find, understand, and use. Then:

- state the answer, decision, or requested action early;
- use familiar words and concrete verbs;
- name the actor, mechanism, and result when known;
- explain a necessary specialist term at first use;
- keep each paragraph focused and use headings or lists only when they aid
  navigation;
- preserve precision, qualifications, citations, uncertainty, and the
  writer's intentional voice.

[PLAIN's practical guidance](https://plainlanguage.com/what-is-plain-language/)
also treats audience and purpose, structure, expression, and evaluation as
parts of the job. A readability score is not proof that the intended reader
can use the text.

## STE-informed technical pass

ASD-STE100 is a controlled language for technical documentation. Its writing
rules and controlled dictionary reduce ambiguity by limiting meanings and
keeping terminology consistent. For a procedure, use the underlying discipline
when it helps:

- put a required condition before the action it controls;
- use one clear action per step;
- name the actor or component instead of relying on an ambiguous pronoun;
- use the same term for the same thing;
- prefer direct, concrete verbs and state the expected result;
- retain necessary technical names, warnings, units, and exact requirements.

The [official ASD-STE100 FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/faq.html) explains the
scope, dictionary, and limits of STE. Consult the official specification when
formal contractual or safety requirements call for it; do not copy its
dictionary into this skill.

## Final check

After the normal `unslop` fidelity and naturalness audit, ask:

1. Can the intended reader find the needed information?
2. Can they understand it without guessing what a term or pronoun means?
3. Can they use it without losing a required condition, qualification, or
   safety constraint?
4. Does the result still sound like the intended writer rather than a generic
   template?

If simplifying would change technical meaning, preserve the precise wording
and explain it instead of replacing it with a weaker synonym.
